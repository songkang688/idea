## 2. Full idea list

### Cluster BOUND — boundary quality & thin structures (SEG-0001–0025)

**SEG-0001 · BOUND** — Decompose boundary error into localization displacement vs. semantic-ownership flips and show they need different fixes.
P: Boundary metrics lump together "contour slightly off" and "pixels assigned to the wrong neighboring class". H: For modern models, most residual boundary error at high resolution is ownership flipping, not geometric blur, and ownership errors respond to context modeling while displacement responds to resolution. W: Determines whether the field should invest in higher-resolution decoders or better relational reasoning. N: No standard decomposition of boundary error by cause exists; boundary metrics are aggregate. E: Build a matched-contour analyzer that classifies each boundary error pixel into displacement/ownership; run on several public models; correlate each component with resolution and context ablations. R: The two error types may be entangled at corners and junctions, making the decomposition ill-defined there.

**SEG-0002 · BOUND** — Measure the human boundary-jitter noise floor per class and test whether SOTA boundary scores are already below it.
P: Boundary-quality papers claim gains without knowing the inter-annotator noise floor. H: For a majority of common classes, differences among top models' boundary F-scores are smaller than inter-annotator boundary jitter, i.e., recent "boundary improvements" partly fit annotator noise. W: Could redirect large research effort away from chasing noise. N: Noise floors are studied for classification labels; a per-class boundary jitter floor with model comparison against it is not standard practice. E: Multi-annotator relabeling of a few hundred boundary segments; compute jitter distribution; compare with inter-model differences. R: Annotator pool may not represent original dataset annotators' skill/protocol.

**SEG-0003 · BOUND** — Train models to predict the distribution of plausible boundary positions (offset distribution) instead of a single contour.
P: A single hard contour ignores that the true boundary is a distribution induced by annotator variation and physical fuzziness. H: A model predicting per-boundary-point offset distributions achieves better expected downstream measurement accuracy (e.g., area estimates) than a point-estimate model with equal capacity. W: Downstream measurements (areas, volumes) need error bars, not lines. N: Distributional regression targeted specifically at boundary offsets, evaluated by measurement-interval coverage, is unexplored. E: Convert multi-rater masks into offset distributions on normals; train small model; check interval coverage of area estimates. R: Multi-rater data scarcity limits the fidelity of the target distribution.

**SEG-0004 · BOUND** — Learn a "break oracle" that predicts where a thin-structure model will produce continuity breaks, then spend targeted compute only there.
P: Vessel/wire/crack segmentation breaks at low-contrast loci; global topology losses are expensive and diffuse. H: Break locations are predictable from local input statistics (contrast, width, curvature) with high recall before running the segmenter, so targeted high-resolution reprocessing of predicted break sites recovers most connectivity errors at a small compute cost. W: Connectivity is the actual utility metric in vasculature, roads, and industrial inspection. N: Treats topology failure as a predictable-event problem rather than a loss-design problem. E: Train a lightweight break predictor from (image, GT skeleton, model errors); measure break recall and connectivity gain from local reprocessing. R: Breaks may be caused by global model state rather than local evidence, making them unpredictable locally.

**SEG-0005 · BOUND** — Sub-pixel boundary evaluation: regress signed-distance residuals and test whether sub-pixel scoring reorders model rankings.
P: Boundary metrics are quantized to the pixel grid; models may differ systematically below one pixel. H: Sub-pixel evaluation (continuous contour vs. continuous contour) changes model rankings relative to pixel-grid boundary F-score, especially across decoder designs. W: Metrology-grade applications (lithography, microscopy, mapping) care about sub-pixel accuracy. N: Sub-pixel systematic bias of segmentation decoders is essentially unmeasured. E: Create high-resolution GT downsampled to test resolution; evaluate models against the high-resolution contour; compare rankings. R: GT itself must be trusted at sub-pixel level, requiring synthetic or metrology data.

**SEG-0006 · BOUND** — Quantify how much boundary-quality improvement actually transfers to downstream task utility, and find the saturation point.
P: Boundary papers assume better boundary F-score is always better. H: For representative downstream tasks (volume measurement, grasping, image editing), utility saturates at a task-specific boundary accuracy threshold beyond which further gains yield no downstream benefit. W: Defines when the boundary problem is "solved enough" per application. N: A boundary-accuracy-to-utility response curve has not been mapped. E: Take one model, synthetically degrade/improve boundaries in controlled steps, measure downstream task metrics. R: Synthetic boundary perturbations may not match the error structure of real models.

**SEG-0007 · BOUND** — Predict thin-structure recall from the Nyquist ratio of structure width to effective network stride, before running the model.
P: Thin structures vanish in downsampling paths. H: Per-structure recall is a predictable function of (structure width / effective stride at deepest resolution-limiting layer), largely architecture-family-invariant, enabling a priori failure prediction from image spectra. W: Lets practitioners choose input resolution/architecture analytically instead of by trial. N: Frames thin-structure failure as a sampling-theory property with a measurable law. E: Vary widths synthetically and stride via input scaling; fit recall vs. Nyquist ratio; test transfer of the fitted curve to real vessels/wires. R: Learned anti-aliasing behavior may make the law model-specific.

**SEG-0008 · BOUND** — Show that high-curvature boundary smoothing is inherited from annotation practice, not architecture.
P: Models systematically round sharp corners and high-curvature details. H: Curvature-conditional error curves are nearly identical across very different architectures trained on the same labels, but change when trained on labels created with a different annotation instrument — implying the bias source is labels. W: Redirects "corner-preserving architecture" work toward label-protocol fixes. N: Attribution of curvature bias to labels vs. models has not been tested by instrument-controlled experiments. E: Relabel a subset with high-fidelity instrument; train same architecture on both label sets; compare curvature-conditional error. R: Expensive relabeling; instrument effects may confound with annotator skill.

**SEG-0009 · BOUND** — Establish correction-click cost as a primary segmentation metric and test whether it disagrees with IoU rankings.
P: Practical mask quality is "how long to fix it", not overlap. H: Ranking models by simulated correction-click count (using a fixed interactive-editing model) disagrees with IoU ranking on a substantial fraction of model pairs, because fragmented errors cost more clicks than smooth ones at equal IoU. W: Aligns benchmarks with annotation/production reality. N: Click-cost as a standardized, simulator-backed metric for non-interactive models is new. E: Build a deterministic correction simulator; evaluate public models; compute rank correlation with IoU. R: Simulator design choices may drive conclusions; needs human validation subset.

**SEG-0010 · BOUND** — Introduce typed boundaries: label every boundary pixel as contact, occlusion, or true-object-edge, and test type-aware training.
P: A mask boundary conflates "object ends here" with "object is occluded here", which have different geometry statistics. H: Models trained with boundary-type supervision produce better amodal completion and better boundary placement than type-agnostic training at equal label budget. W: Occlusion reasoning is a core failure of dense prediction. N: Boundary typing as a first-class annotation and supervision target (not amodal masks per se) is underexplored. E: Annotate types on an existing dataset subset (cheap: type per boundary segment); train with/without type head; evaluate amodal and modal metrics. R: Type labels may be ambiguous at complex junctions.

**SEG-0011 · BOUND** — Measure how boundary errors compound through multi-stage pipelines (segment → crop → refine → measure).
P: Cascaded systems assume stage errors are independent; boundary errors may amplify. H: Boundary displacement errors compound superlinearly through cropping-based stages because crops computed from wrong boundaries truncate evidence needed by later stages. W: Most production systems are cascades; error budgets are guessed today. N: A quantitative propagation model for dense-prediction cascades is missing. E: Instrument a 3-stage pipeline; inject controlled boundary perturbations at stage 1; fit propagation curves. R: Findings may be pipeline-specific; need several pipeline archetypes.

**SEG-0012 · BOUND** — Test whether annotation instrument (polygon vs. brush vs. model-assisted) changes model architecture rankings.
P: Datasets silently mix annotation instruments; instruments impose different boundary statistics. H: The ranking of architectures by boundary metrics flips depending on which instrument produced the training and test labels, i.e., part of "architecture quality" is instrument fit. W: Undermines cross-paper comparisons; informs dataset design. N: Instrument as an experimental factor in model comparison is essentially untested. E: Relabel one subset three ways; train 3 architectures on each; test on each; analyze interaction term. R: 9-cell experiment is annotation-heavy; use small but well-powered subset.

**SEG-0013 · BOUND** — Calibrated signed-distance fields: output per-pixel distance-to-boundary with valid error bars.
P: Distance-to-boundary is what planners and measurement tools consume, but models output uncalibrated masks. H: A model trained to output distance with heteroscedastic uncertainty achieves valid coverage (stated intervals contain true distance) even where mask IoU is mediocre, because distance uncertainty is easier to calibrate than pixel class. W: Enables safety margins with guarantees (surgical margins, robot clearance). N: Calibration of geometric distance rather than class probability is a different target than standard calibration work. E: Train distance head with quantile loss; evaluate interval coverage vs. distance from boundary across datasets. R: Coverage may break exactly in the rare far-off-boundary failure cases that matter.

**SEG-0014 · BOUND** — Characterize late-training boundary churn: interior pixels converge early while boundary pixels keep oscillating and drive overfitting.
P: Training dynamics of dense models are analyzed globally, not spatially. H: After an early epoch, nearly all gradient signal and all memorization-type overfitting concentrates within a few pixels of boundaries; freezing interior-pixel loss late in training preserves accuracy and improves boundary generalization. W: Suggests spatially scheduled training as a general recipe and explains boundary overfitting. N: Spatially resolved training-dynamics analysis for segmentation is rare. E: Log per-pixel loss/gradient by distance-to-boundary across training; run the interior-freezing intervention. R: Effect may be dataset-size dependent and vanish at scale.

**SEG-0015 · BOUND** — A decomposed boundary benchmark: report misalignment, topology, ownership, and fragmentation as four separate scores.
P: Single boundary scores hide which failure a method fixes. H: Existing "boundary-improvement" methods each improve mostly one component; decomposed reporting reveals that no current method improves topology without hurting alignment. W: Turns boundary research from leaderboard chasing into failure-targeted engineering. N: An adopted decomposition standard does not exist. E: Implement the four-component scorer; re-evaluate public checkpoints; publish component matrix. R: Components must be provably near-orthogonal or reviewers will dispute the decomposition.

**SEG-0016 · BOUND** — Test whether sequential tracing (agent walks along the structure) beats dense prediction for thin-structure connectivity at matched compute.
P: Humans trace vessels/roads sequentially; networks predict all pixels at once, breaking connectivity. H: At matched FLOPs, a learned tracer that conditions each step on its own path achieves strictly better connectivity metrics (fewer breaks) but worse area overlap than dense prediction — a measurable structural trade-off. W: Identifies which paradigm suits connectivity-critical applications. N: A controlled dense-vs-tracing comparison with compute matching is missing; tracing is treated as legacy. E: Implement a minimal recurrent tracer and matched dense model on vessel + road data; compare connectivity vs. overlap. R: Tracer training instability could confound the paradigm comparison.

**SEG-0017 · BOUND** — Represent boundaries as splines with per-control-point uncertainty to localize ambiguity along the contour.
P: Pixel-wise uncertainty maps smear boundary ambiguity; users need "this segment of the contour is uncertain". H: Contour-parametric uncertainty (per control point) predicts human correction locations better than pixel-entropy maps do. W: Directly supports efficient human review of contours. N: Uncertainty attached to contour parameters rather than pixels is rarely evaluated against human edit behavior. E: Fit spline heads with uncertainty on an interactive dataset; compare predicted-uncertain segments with logged human edits. R: Splines struggle with topology changes; scope to simple closed contours first.

**SEG-0018 · BOUND** — Fit per-class power laws of boundary quality vs. input resolution and use them to prescribe minimum resolutions.
P: Choosing inference resolution is folklore. H: Boundary F-score follows a saturating power law in resolution with class-specific exponents predictable from class boundary tortuosity, enabling analytic resolution prescriptions. W: Saves compute and prevents silent under-resolution failures. N: Resolution scaling laws are studied for classification, not for boundary quality per class. E: Evaluate several models across a resolution ladder; fit and cross-validate laws; test prescription on a held-out dataset. R: Laws may not transfer across architectures with different striding.

**SEG-0019 · BOUND** — Quantify evidence-free boundary hallucination: how much of the predicted contour persists when the underlying evidence is masked out.
P: Models complete plausible boundaries from shape priors even where the image contains no evidence (occlusion, blur). H: A measurable fraction of predicted boundary length is "evidence-free" (survives counterfactual local masking), and this fraction predicts out-of-distribution boundary errors better than confidence does. W: Hallucinated boundaries are dangerous in medicine and mapping precisely because they look confident. N: Counterfactual evidence-attribution applied specifically to boundary pixels is new as a metric. E: Systematically mask local patches; measure contour stability; correlate evidence-free fraction with OOD error. R: Masking introduces its own distribution shift; needs careful counterfactual design (inpainting-based masking).

**SEG-0020 · BOUND** — Detect annotation-tool artifacts (polygon corner statistics) leaking into model predictions.
P: Polygon labels have angular spectra that natural object contours lack. H: Trained models' predicted contours exhibit the angular signature of the annotation tool (vertex-like corners at tool-typical spacing), demonstrating direct label-artifact learning; the signature strength predicts cross-dataset boundary transfer gap. W: A concrete, measurable channel of dataset bias. N: Spectral forensics of predicted contours against annotation-tool signatures is unexplored. E: Compute curvature/angle spectra of GT vs. predictions vs. natural high-res contours; compare across datasets with different tools. R: Signature may be too weak after augmentation smoothing.

**SEG-0021 · BOUND** — Test whether boundary localization ability transfers across classes independently of class semantics.
P: Few-shot models fail at boundaries of novel classes; unclear if boundary skill is class-generic. H: A decoder's boundary-localization component, frozen after training on class set A, loses almost no boundary accuracy on disjoint class set B (given correct interior), i.e., boundary skill is class-agnostic and should be factored out of few-shot adaptation. W: Would restructure few-shot segmentation into interior-semantics adaptation + universal boundary module. E: Train on A; freeze boundary-refinement pathway; supervise only interior on B; measure boundary metrics vs. full fine-tuning. N: Explicit factorization test of boundary vs. semantics transfer is missing. R: Architectures may not cleanly expose a "boundary pathway" to freeze.

**SEG-0022 · BOUND** — Audit whether models exploit compression artifacts co-located with object boundaries as a shortcut.
P: JPEG block/ringing artifacts correlate with high-contrast edges in web datasets. H: Models trained on compressed data lose disproportionate boundary accuracy on uncompressed/RAW test images, and the loss is recoverable by matched-compression augmentation — demonstrating a compression shortcut. W: Datasets are silently compressed; deployment sensors are not. N: Compression as a boundary-specific shortcut (not just robustness issue) is untested. E: Train on compressed vs. recompressed-matched data; test on RAW captures; decompose error by distance-to-boundary. R: Hard to obtain paired RAW test data with labels.

**SEG-0023 · BOUND** — Use equivariance-violation under augmentation as a label-free per-image boundary-quality estimate.
P: There is no way to know boundary quality at deployment without labels. H: The disagreement between boundaries predicted under small geometric transforms (after inverse-mapping) correlates strongly with true per-image boundary error, providing a deployable quality gauge, and the correlation is stable across domains. W: Enables triage/review routing in production. N: Equivariance violation as a calibrated boundary-quality estimator (not a training loss) is largely unstudied. E: Compute violation vs. true boundary error over multiple datasets/shifts; fit and validate a quality predictor. R: Model may be equivariantly wrong (consistent but incorrect), capping achievable correlation.

**SEG-0024 · BOUND** — Learn asymmetric safety-margin boundaries: never-undersegment or never-oversegment guarantees as constrained optimization.
P: Clinical/robotic uses need one-sided errors (cover all tumor; never claim free space that isn't). H: Training with an explicit one-sided constraint (e.g., quantile-style boundary offsets) achieves target one-sided violation rates with far less area inflation than post-hoc dilation. W: One-sided guarantees are what safety cases actually require. N: Direct constrained learning of asymmetric boundary offsets with validated violation rates is not standard. E: Train with one-sided losses at several target rates; compare violation-rate/area-inflation frontier against dilation baselines. R: Violation-rate validity under domain shift is the crux; must test under shift.

**SEG-0025 · BOUND** — Train against continuous vector supervision (implicit contour fields) to eliminate rasterization bias, and measure the systematic sub-pixel offset it removes.
P: Vector annotations are rasterized before training, injecting a half-pixel-scale systematic bias. H: Models supervised directly on continuous contour fields (signed distance defined from the vector source) show a measurable reduction of systematic contour offset vs. raster-supervised twins. W: Matters wherever masks feed measurements. N: The rasterization step as a bias source has not been isolated experimentally. E: Same data, raster vs. vector-field supervision; measure mean signed contour offset against high-precision GT. R: The bias may be negligible relative to annotation noise (a valuable negative result, but less exciting).

### Cluster SMALL — small objects & scale (SEG-0026–0045)

**SEG-0026 · SMALL** — Determine the per-task pixel budget at which small-object recognition switches from object-evidence to context-inference.
P: It is unclear whether small-object failure is a resolution problem or a recognition problem. H: Below a class-specific pixel budget, model decisions are driven predominantly by context (verified by context-scrambling), so super-resolving the object does not help while context editing flips the prediction. W: Tells the field whether to invest in resolution, context modeling, or data. N: The evidence-source switch point has not been measured per class. E: Paste objects at controlled sizes into congruent vs. scrambled contexts; measure recall vs. size per condition. R: Pasting artifacts may themselves act as cues; needs blending controls.

**SEG-0027 · SMALL** — Test whether small-object AP is label-noise-bound by re-annotating small instances with high precision.
P: Small-object boxes/masks have proportionally huge annotation error. H: A large fraction of the gap between small-object and large-object AP reflects annotation noise (both missing labels and geometric jitter), and careful re-annotation of the test set raises measured small AP substantially without touching the models. W: Field-wide small-object efforts may be aimed at a partly fictitious gap. N: A noise-corrected small-object benchmark does not exist. E: Precisely re-annotate small objects in a benchmark subset (zoom tooling, multi-rater); re-evaluate public detectors. R: Expensive annotation; must control for re-annotation introducing new conventions.

**SEG-0028 · SMALL** — Causally test gradient starvation: does the tiny loss share of small objects, rather than feature quality, limit small-object learning?
P: Small objects contribute few pixels/anchors, hence little gradient. H: Equalizing per-object (not per-pixel) gradient contribution during training changes the optimization path and improves small recall at fixed architecture, showing the bottleneck is optimization-signal allocation, not representational capacity. W: Redirects work from architecture to training-signal accounting. N: Loss reweighting exists, but a controlled causal study of gradient share → outcome with per-object normalization and gradient measurements is missing. E: Instrument per-object gradient norms; train matched runs with equalized shares; compare recall by size decile. R: Equalization may destabilize training; requires careful tuning to keep comparison fair.

**SEG-0029 · SMALL** — Measure assignment churn: small objects flip matcher assignments across training and this instability predicts final misses.
P: Anchor/query matching for small objects is noisy. H: The per-object frequency of assignment flips early in training predicts which objects the final model misses, better than object size itself; stabilizing assignments for high-churn objects recovers part of small AP. W: Points to matching, an unglamorous component, as a primary small-object bottleneck. N: Assignment churn as a measurable, predictive quantity is unstudied. E: Log matcher assignments per GT object per epoch; correlate churn with final outcome; intervene with churn-damped matching. R: Churn may be symptom, not cause; the intervention arm is essential.

**SEG-0030 · SMALL** — Unify detection and density estimation with a principled hand-off governed by per-region resolvability.
P: In dense tiny-object scenes (birds, cells, crowds) detection collapses but counting still works; systems pick one paradigm globally. H: A model that outputs instances where resolvable and calibrated density where not, with the hand-off governed by a learned resolvability score, beats both pure paradigms on a combined count+localization metric. W: Real applications need graceful degradation from instances to counts. N: The resolvability-governed hand-off and its joint metric are new as a formal task. E: Build joint metric; train hand-off model on crowd/cell data; compare with detection-only and density-only. R: The combined metric design could be contested; needs axiomatic grounding.

**SEG-0031 · SMALL** — Show small objects are memorized, not generalized, via membership inference conditioned on object size.
P: Few training examples exist per small-object appearance; generalization is assumed anyway. H: Membership-inference success on training images is markedly higher when queried on small-object regions than large-object regions, indicating memorization concentrates on small instances — with privacy and augmentation implications. W: Privacy risk and a hint that small-object "learning" is partly lookup. N: Size-conditioned memorization analysis for dense tasks is unexplored. E: Run region-conditioned membership inference on detectors/segmenters; compare by size decile. R: Membership-inference signals are weak; needs large sample sizes.

**SEG-0032 · SMALL** — Quantify what fraction of reported small-object failures are sliding-window/tiling artifacts rather than model failures.
P: High-res inference uses tiling; objects at tile seams fail for pipeline reasons. H: A nontrivial share of benchmark small-object misses are seam-position-dependent (they disappear when the tiling grid is shifted), meaning reported small AP conflates model quality with inference protocol. W: Cleans up evaluation and could "free" performance with protocol fixes. N: Seam-attribution of failures has not been systematically measured. E: Evaluate with multiple tiling offsets; classify misses as seam-dependent vs. stable; report per benchmark. R: Compute-heavy; conclusions specific to tiling-based pipelines.

**SEG-0033 · SMALL** — Measure the context-to-object evidence ratio as a function of object size and link it to hallucination in unusual contexts.
P: Detectors detect small objects "because of where they are". H: Below a size threshold, occlusion-based attribution shows context contributes the majority of decision evidence, and this context share quantitatively predicts false positives in context-congruent but object-absent scenes. W: Explains and predicts dangerous hallucinations (e.g., phantom pedestrians at crosswalks). N: A quantitative evidence-share curve vs. size, tied causally to hallucination rates, is missing. E: Attribution via masking on size-stratified detections; construct object-absent congruent scenes; correlate. R: Attribution methods are imperfect; triangulate with two attribution families.

**SEG-0034 · SMALL** — Characterize the false-positive typology introduced by super-resolution preprocessing for small-object detection.
P: SR improves small AP but may invent structures. H: SR preprocessing increases a specific FP class — texture-consistent hallucinated objects — whose rate grows with SR upscaling factor, and this cost is invisible in AP but decisive in safety terms. W: SR is widely deployed as a "free" fix. N: FP typology accounting for SR pipelines is missing; AP hides it. E: Run detectors with/without SR; human-audit new FPs; build typology and rate curves vs. scale factor. R: Typology labeling is subjective; use multiple auditors and adjudication.

**SEG-0035 · SMALL** — Audit scale equivariance precisely in the small regime and predict per-object failure from feature scale-response curves.
P: CNN/ViT features are only approximately scale-equivariant, worst at small scales. H: A model's per-class scale-response curve (feature similarity under rescaling), measured once, predicts which object sizes will fail, transferring across datasets — turning small-object failure into a measurable model property. W: A cheap diagnostic replacing expensive per-dataset evaluation. N: Scale-response as a predictive per-model fingerprint is unexploited. E: Compute response curves on probe stimuli; predict size-stratified recall on held-out datasets; measure predictive accuracy. R: Interaction with context may dominate, weakening pure-scale prediction.

**SEG-0036 · SMALL** — Standardize "minimum resolvable size" (MRS) as per-class dataset metadata and test whether MRS mismatch explains cross-dataset transfer failure.
P: Datasets differ in effective ground-sample distance and object-size distributions; transfer results confound this. H: A large share of cross-dataset small-object transfer gap is explained by MRS mismatch alone, and resampling images to align MRS closes much of the gap without any adaptation method. W: A trivial, deployable fix if true; better dataset documentation regardless. N: MRS as standardized metadata and as an explanatory variable is new. E: Compute MRS per dataset; run transfer matrix with/without MRS alignment; variance decomposition. R: MRS alignment by resampling degrades other aspects (context field of view).

**SEG-0037 · SMALL** — Learn where to spend high-resolution compute: adaptive zoom policies with an explicit accuracy-per-FLOP objective.
P: Uniform high-res inference wastes compute where nothing small exists. H: A learned zoom policy spending a 10–20% high-res budget recovers most of the full-res small-object AP, and the achievable frontier is predictable from a cheap saliency of "small-object likelihood". W: Makes small-object detection affordable at deployment scale. N: Policy work exists in classification; a rigorous accuracy-per-FLOP frontier for small-object dense prediction with learned policies is underdeveloped. E: Train a two-pass zoom policy; map the budget-accuracy frontier vs. uniform and heuristic baselines. R: Policy may overfit dataset layout priors (e.g., objects near horizon).

**SEG-0038 · SMALL** — Benchmark "sub-visible" object detection from temporal integration: objects invisible in any single frame but present in motion energy.
P: Single-frame detection is the default even for video, discarding integration gains that biology uses. H: For sub-threshold objects (distant drones, birds), temporal-integration models detect a substantial fraction of objects that single-frame models cannot at any threshold, and time-to-detection scales predictably with contrast. W: Early detection of distant hazards is safety-critical. N: A benchmark defined by single-frame invisibility (psychophysically verified) does not exist. E: Collect/simulate sequences with calibrated sub-visible targets; compare single-frame vs. temporal models on time-to-detection. R: Ground truth for "invisible" objects requires instrumented capture.

**SEG-0039 · SMALL** — Replace box IoU with a continuous center+scale error metric for tiny objects and test for ranking flips.
P: Box IoU is quantized and near-degenerate below ~10 px; a 1-px shift swings IoU wildly. H: A metric based on normalized center offset and log-scale error re-ranks detectors on tiny-object benchmarks relative to AP, and correlates better with downstream tracking performance. W: Metric artifacts may be steering tiny-object research. N: The degeneracy is known folklore; a validated replacement metric with downstream-correlation evidence is missing. E: Implement metric; re-evaluate public detectors; correlate both metrics with tracking success on the same data. R: Community adoption inertia; must show clear practical superiority.

**SEG-0040 · SMALL** — Test for representational collapse across feature-pyramid levels: are "small-object features" just aliased copies of mid-scale features?
P: FPN-style designs assume levels specialize by scale. H: CKA/similarity analysis conditioned on object size shows high-level redundancy: the levels serving small objects encode little scale-specific information, and enforcing decorrelation between levels improves small AP without capacity change. W: Explains why deeper pyramids underdeliver for small objects. N: Size-conditioned representational-similarity analysis of pyramids is unstudied. E: Measure inter-level CKA on size-stratified object features across detectors; run decorrelation intervention. R: Similarity metrics can mislead; support with probing tasks.
